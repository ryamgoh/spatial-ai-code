# External human spatial-QA capability review

Status: primary-source review, 2026-10-10. This is an evidence and proposed-admission note, **not an implemented benchmark adapter, semantic filter, or capability-labelled evaluation**. Paper reading, official-code inspection, release inventory checks, and limited lexical/sample inspection were performed. **Automated semantic filtering, adaptation, answer-equivalence checking, and RR/LR/MR tagging have not been run.** No retained/excluded totals or capability frequencies are reported.

Terminology note: `MR` was the proposal's working shorthand during this review.
The consolidated proposal now uses **MTR (Model-Theoretic Reasoning)** to avoid
collision with SODA's Mental Rotation task; no mapping conclusion changes.

## Conclusion and claim boundary

SpartQA-Human and ReSQ are defensible **external tests of natural spatial language and relational transfer under their native output contracts**. Neither reviewed source establishes coverage of SpatialEntail's complete proposed LR/MR suite. SpartQA has an explicit unknown answer; ReSQ is binary. Both involve relations and language outside the supported point-compass fragment. A directional, individually audited subset is a proposed evaluation, not a presently verified alignment. In particular, “reasoning,” quantifier words, and implication rules inside a dataset's reasoner do not establish visible propositional IF/OR/IFF premises. [S1 §3, §5; R1 §3.1, §5.1.1; local T, V]

Use three different claims in reporting:

- **Directional transfer:** performance on native directional questions, with native grading, after reporting the surrounding ontology/language. This need not imply solver compatibility.
- **Tagged semantic subset:** a future subset whose complete effective context, query, answer semantics, and necessary capabilities have passed a declared adapter/audit. Any rewriting is disclosed separately from native evaluation.
- **Unsupported evidence:** do not claim a capability because an answer label, task name, lexical token, or internal spatial-rule implementation resembles it. Lack of evidence in this review is not proof that no release example could ever be annotated that way. [S1 §3–§5; R1 §3.1, §4, Table 2; T “Evidence and feasibility gates”]

## Native output semantics: do not replace these silently

### SpartQA-Human

The **observed release encodings** are: FR ordered candidates `["left","right","above","below","near to","far from","touching","DK"]` with zero-based integer answer indices; YN answer lists `["Yes"]`, `["No"]`, `["DK"]` (its release `candidate_answers` field can be an empty string, so the domain is established from paper/code, not assumed from that field); CO `[object1 phrase, object2 phrase, "both of them", "none of them"]` with an answer index; FB offered block names with answers as block-string lists, including multiple names or an empty list. FB candidates vary with available blocks, rather than universally being A/B/C. The baseline encodes these separately and returns selected FR union labels (pruning conflicting LEFT/RIGHT, ABOVE/BELOW and NEAR/FAR pairs by the higher positive logit) or fallback `[7]` (DK), FB selected block names or `[]`, CO both `[2]`/none `[3]`, and ternary YN. **FR DK is not an exact compass alternative and FB empty selection is not unknown.** [S2 archive; S2 [`BERT.py` L63–93](https://github.com/HLR/SpartQA-baselines/blob/39dce5877f5411ec85866025ba8c5aafa1bca2b1/BERT.py#L63-L93), [L125–129](https://github.com/HLR/SpartQA-baselines/blob/39dce5877f5411ec85866025ba8c5aafa1bca2b1/BERT.py#L125-L129), [L147–178](https://github.com/HLR/SpartQA-baselines/blob/39dce5877f5411ec85866025ba8c5aafa1bca2b1/BERT.py#L147-L178), [L224–230](https://github.com/HLR/SpartQA-baselines/blob/39dce5877f5411ec85866025ba8c5aafa1bca2b1/BERT.py#L224-L230)]

Simultaneously true LEFT and ABOVE, for example, differ from the alternative possibilities {Northwest, North, Northeast}. | **CO** | Choose which of two described objects meets the requested spatial condition, with both/neither outcomes. [S1 §3 pp.4584–4585; Appendix Tables 7–8 pp.4595–4596] | Finite object membership/selection may motivate a future QAG/Which projection, but source “Both” is not an integer Count answer and neither is not DK. No CNT/JCR coverage follows. |
| **FB** | Find block(s) containing the described object(s). [S1 §3 pp.4584–4585; Appendix Tables 7–8 pp.4595–4596] | The paper-defined task is containment selection, **not compass Which**, and is unsupported by the present spatial ontology without an extension. Important restriction on interpreting **No**: S1 §4's answer-generation procedure finds the queried relation for Yes, the inverse relation for No, otherwise DK. That procedure is explicitly for **SpartQA-Auto**. It is helpful evidence of the authors' intended distinction between negative and missing information, but is not a complete Human formal semantics or a general theorem that every negated relation equals its converse. Preserve Human gold answers and audit them independently. [S1 §4, “Answer generation,” p.4586; V “Premises,” “Semantic assessment”]

The paper discusses near/far disagreement and excludes those relations from its FR evaluations. The official baseline explicitly removes raw indices `4` and `5` (near/far) from gold and predicted FR lists for its separate distance-free accuracy. That scoring choice does not remove distance from the source's story language or relation ontology, and is not permission to delete metric premises in a V2 projection. A reproduction must specify whether it uses the paper's restricted relation scoring or the released native candidate/answer lists. [S1 §6.1 pp.4587–4588; S2 [`QA/test.py` L221–228](https://github.com/HLR/SpartQA-baselines/blob/39dce5877f5411ec85866025ba8c5aafa1bca2b1/QA/test.py#L221-L228)]

### ReSQ

ReSQ consists of volunteer-authored **Yes/No questions** on existing MSPRL image descriptions. R1 §3.1 defines the binary YN task; §5.1.1 and Appendix A.2 specifically describe ReSQ's question collection. The official release has `q_type: "YN"`, `candidate_answers: ["Yes", "No"]`, and gold lists such as `["Yes"]` or `["No"]`; the baseline's `sprlqa` route encodes and scores those two labels. **There is no native DK label to which SpatialEntail contingency can be mapped.** [R1 §3.1 p.6150, §5.1.1 p.6154, A.2 p.6160; R2 `ReSQ/*_resq.json`; R3 `Create_LM_input_output.py` L65–74, L101–114, `QA/sprlqa/test.py` L90–101]

**No is a native binary annotation, not a demonstrated closed-world formal entailment rule.** The inspected paper and classification code establish the answer domain; they do not supply an all-model contract proving that every No is an impossible claim in a point-compass theory. For a future supported projection with consistent premises P and claim q, independently check both P∧q and P∧¬q: Yes when q is entailed; No when q is impossible; contingent when both conjunctions are satisfiable (in different models); inconsistent when neither conjunction is satisfiable. Contingent/inconsistent projected rows are out of the native binary contract, to be disclosed or excluded—not coerced into No or an invented third native label. Native binary model evaluation can still be reported without pretending this formal projection already exists. [R1 §3.1, §5.1.1, A.2; R3 cited classifier; V “Semantic assessment”]

## Ontology, language and directly observed examples

### Relations and representation mismatch

SpartQA's task definitions include relative directions, near/far distance, touching and containing. ReSQ's observed questions additionally include inside, disconnected, touching, front/behind, near, above/below, and left/right. R1 Table 1's broader spatial-language framework distinguishes RCC8 topology/proper parts, relative directions including FRONT/BEHIND, and qualitative FAR/NEAR. Do **not** infer that every Table 1 relation is represented in ReSQ or that every ReSQ question has the synthetic generator's formal annotations. [S1 §3 p.4584; R1 Table 1 p.6149; R2 examples below]

V2's **North** requires equal X and strict northward Y, whereas **Northward** leaves X unconstrained. Thus source ABOVE does not warrant singleton North, and LEFT does not warrant singleton West. A potential named-object abstraction is ABOVE→Northward, BELOW→Southward, LEFT→Westward, RIGHT→Eastward, but this is a **proposed abstraction requiring semantic validation**, not an established equivalence for regions/extended objects. Joint LEFT+ABOVE predicates could support exact Northwest *after* the abstraction is validated; their appearance alone does not prove independently derived axes (AXC). [V “World model,” “Premises”; S1 §3, §5]

The contrast is concrete in SpaRTUN code: [`Find_Relations_type.py`, `obj_obj_relation`, L125–161](https://github.com/HLR/SpaRTUN/blob/ab924d2324ac28955cc1b72b7edf1bbd5b35e811/Find_Relations_type.py#L125-L161) tests X/size and Y/size independently; LEFT does not require Y equality. Its metric/topology tests also use object extents. This is evidence about **SpaRTUN's generator**, not a recovered formal semantics for every SpartQA-Human or ReSQ sentence. [R2 cited file]

## Proposed filtering, tagging and excluded-subset accounting

This is a **protocol proposal only**. The adapter and automated semantic filter have not been run. The policy follows the target's explicit supported fragment and the observed native contract mismatches above. [T; V; S1 §3; R1 §3.1, §5.1.1; R2 records; R3 context handling]

### Conservative admission and reporting

1. **Keep native and adapted evaluations separate.** Preserve original text, native task, candidates, answer, split, file/hash/revision and item locator. Report native Yes/No/DK and native relation-label/selection accuracy without changing unknown to false. Record every rewrite and target query contract separately.
2. **Resolve effective context and references first.** ReSQ baseline `QA/sprlqa/test.py` L48–63 loads `*_sprlqa.json` and joins `story[:num_1st_context_sentences+1]`, while the author archive names files `*_resq.json`. Resolve filename and context-index conventions rather than assuming the field is a validated sentence-count specification. Preserve both source field and actual effective context. Do not use later story information unnoticed. [R2 archive; R3 cited file]
3. **Prefer a whole-effective-context conservative filter.** Admit only unambiguously referenced, finite named entities with supported planar relations/formulas and no semantic dependence on quantification, containment, topology, metric/size rules, front/behind, or commonsense. Object colour/shape as identity descriptions is not automatically a spatial-size rule, but unresolved reference is an exclusion. Unknown/null metadata does not establish absence of commonsense.
4. **Do not retain a row merely because its question says left/above.** Unsupported relevant premises cannot be silently removed. A query-support-only directional projection would require an independent native-ontology semantic-equivalence check between the full context and the projection, plus dropped-premise/rewrite provenance. No such check was implemented in this review; use whole-context exclusion until it exists.
5. **Translate coarse axes without inventing equality.** Where justified by an item-level semantic audit, use coarse direction domains rather than singleton cardinal atoms. For FR, compare simultaneous entailed source predicates—not `possible_directions` as if its alternatives were multiple true relations. A binary entailment projection tests a supported claim but is not native DIR transfer. State any discarded relation-label dimensions and do not label the rewritten score native FR exact-match.
