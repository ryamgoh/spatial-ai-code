## Motivation

Spatial reasoning requires distinguishing guaranteed answers from possible
answers while composing relations correctly. SpatialEntail will provide
controlled problems, verified labels, and auditable explanations for these
capabilities. External transfer remains an empirical question; easy synthetic
problems or fast training do not guarantee it.

## Reasoning Core

Use two core operations over symbolic axis comparisons:

1. **Decompose/recombine:** Northeast(X,Y) means `x(X)>x(Y)` and `y(X)>y(Y)`.
2. **Propagate:** transitivity of inequalities, plus equality substitution.

**Side note on inversion:** We considered inversion as a separate operation,
but it is unnecessary once relations are expressed as axis comparisons:
`x(X)>x(Y)` and `x(Y)<x(X)` encode the same ordering. This does not require
sorting all objects or assume the premises determine a complete ordering.
Distinct points may share one coordinate, and some pairwise orders remain unknown.

Preserve the current exact-direction semantics: North means equal x and
greater y. Northeast is not the conjunction of exact North and exact East.

Forward chaining derives definite relations and records a proof graph.
It must not infer an ordering between two objects merely because both are
east of a third object.

Normalize equivalent constraints internally for proof construction and
verification; retain varied premise orientations in model inputs. An explicit
inverse-relation rewrite is optional, not a required proof step. Match any
displayed rewrites across Natural and Symbolic explanations.

## Spatial Assumptions

- Named objects are distinct points in a shared 2D plane with fixed global axes.
- No two objects occupy the same point; sharing one coordinate is allowed.
- Relations encode exact directions, not distances or equal-length steps.
- No implicit grid spacing, map boundary, or distance limit. Computational
  coordinate bounds must not change the semantic answer.
- Relations are open-world: unstated relations are unknown, not false.
- Include consistent and inconsistent premise sets, distinguished explicitly.
- Selection and Count have explicit candidate sets excluding the reference.
  A bounded candidate domain does not imply closed-world relation semantics.

## What “Entailed” Means

For consistent premises, an answer is entailed only if it holds in **every**
map satisfying them. All checks below include the declared spatial assumptions,
including distinct points. Keep Z3 as the correctness oracle:

- Check whole-map consistency first. Unsatisfiable premises receive metadata
  status `INCONSISTENT` and final answer E under every contract, even if the
  conflict is unrelated to the query. Solver errors/timeouts are not evidence
  of inconsistency.
- Claim Q is entailed when `premises AND NOT Q` is unsatisfiable.
- Claim Q is possible when `premises AND Q` is satisfiable.
- Certify underdetermination with two valid maps yielding different answers.

The **counterexample check** asks whether all premises can hold while Q is
false. SAT returns a counterexample map, disproving entailment; UNSAT establishes
entailment; UNKNOWN/timeout establishes neither. A counterexample alone does not
prove ambiguity: Q might be impossible. Numerical witness coordinates demonstrate
valid orderings, not extra distance or unit-step assumptions.

Equivalently, P entails Q when `NOT P OR Q` is valid under the spatial axioms,
not merely satisfiable. Forward chaining starts from P and applies sound rules;
it does not establish entailment by simply processing `NOT P OR Q`.

For contradictions, retain premise IDs forming a verified conflicting subset
and a short explanation, including any spatial axioms used. Do not claim the
subset is minimal or minimum-size. Not every cycle is contradictory: include
matched consistent-cycle controls alongside inconsistent examples.

Failure of forward chaining alone is not an ambiguity certificate. Count
answers require jointly feasible memberships, not sums of independent
possibilities. Selection requires a uniquely justified candidate, not just
one candidate that could satisfy the relation.

## Proof Construction and Verification

Use two complementary components:

| Component | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Axis-graph reasoning** | Construct transparent deductions and supporting paths for Natural/Symbolic explanations. |
| **Z3 verification** | Check whole-map consistency, entailment, possibilities, witness/counterexample maps, and joint Count feasibility. |

For the graph component, group known-equal coordinates separately on each axis,
represent strict orderings as directed edges, and use reachability to compose
constraints. Detect strict cycles, including strict edges within an equality
group. Retain premise provenance for every deduction and displayed conflict.

A sound forward-chain derivation establishes entailment, but failure to derive
a claim does not disprove it unless completeness is established. Do not assume
basic axis closure handles every obligation: no-coincident-points constraints
couple the axes, and Selection/Count require candidate-level and joint checks.
Validate generated evidence against the full premises and spatial axioms.

Retain Z3 as the existing practical verifier, not as a claim that it is the
fastest or uniquely best solver. Internal solver logic does not require Boolean
premises in the dataset. Simplify the visible problem language and proof
construction; a solver replacement is outside the three-week scope.

## Query Types and Answer Contracts

**Query types:** Direction, Selection, Count.
**Answer contracts:** how possible answers and menu coverage are handled.

| Reader-facing name | Existing code value | Required answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Unique Answer** | `single` | The uniquely guaranteed answer; otherwise distinguish ambiguity from no answer. |
| **Listed Possibilities** | `visible-possible` | Every possible substantive answer displayed in the menu. |
| **Exhaustive Possibilities** | `all-possible` | Select the complete possible-answer set when representable in A-D; otherwise use the defined escape. |

Let P be the possible-answer set and M the substantive menu values.
Listed Possibilities targets `P intersect M`; Exhaustive Possibilities targets
`P`. The six-option interface below encodes these sets or an escape, not
unlisted-value generation. For Direction and Count, Unique Answer returns the sole value when
`|P| = 1`. Selection additionally requires that candidate to qualify in every
valid map and all other eligible candidates never qualify.

Include no-match and multiple-certain-match Selection cases in Unique Answer
views. They return E because no uniquely qualifying object exists, not because
the memberships must be uncertain. Record these reasons separately from
underdetermination and inconsistency. A uniquely guaranteed but unlisted answer
returns F. An empty Listed Possibilities result also returns F, not E.

Keep the existing code identifiers; no code-wide rename is required.
Possibility is existential, not entailment of the answer itself: retain
satisfying witnesses for included values and solver checks excluding others.

## Six-Option Interface

Every new prompt displays exactly six options: four distinct substantive
values at **A-D**, plus fixed escapes **E** and **F**. Keep both escape labels
fixed within each contract, never chosen according to an example's gold answer.

| Contract | E label | F label |
|---|---|---|
| Unique Answer | Cannot be determined | None of the above |
| Listed Possibilities | Cannot be determined | None of the above |
| Exhaustive Possibilities | Cannot be determined | No complete answer can be formed from A-D. |

The instructions define their use precisely:

- **All contracts:** check consistency first; contradictory premises return E.
- **Unique:** on consistent premises, select the one A-D letter for the
  uniquely guaranteed answer. Select F if that unique answer is unlisted;
  select E if there is no uniquely guaranteed answer. For Selection this
  includes no qualifying object, multiple certain matches, and variable
  memberships that prevent one object from being the only match in every map.
- **Listed:** on consistent premises, select all A-D letters whose values are
  possible. Select F if none is possible, whether the full possibility set
  is empty or all possible values are unlisted.
- **Exhaustive:** on consistent premises, select all correct A-D letters only
  when they represent the complete nonempty possible set. Select F if any
  possible value is missing, or if the full set is empty (an explicit convention).

Multiple possible answers are not a reason for E under Listed or Exhaustive.
For Count, zero is an ordinary substantive value: the possible set `{0}` is
not empty. For Selection, a lone possible candidate is not necessarily
guaranteed; apply the uniqueness condition above.

**Four menu slots do not limit how many objects can qualify.** Eligible objects
are the objects the question asks us to consider, not necessarily correct or
listed answers. For example, let the candidate set be U, V, W, X, Y:

```text
Consider a map with multiple objects:
U is to the North of O.
V is to the North of O.
W is to the North of O.
X is to the North of O.
Y is to the North of O.
```

All five objects are guaranteed north of O. If A-D display U, V, W, X:

| Selection contract | Final selection | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Unique | E | Five objects qualify, so no single object is the only match. |
| Listed | A, B, C, D | All four listed objects qualify. |
| Exhaustive | F alone | Y is missing; no complete answer can be formed from A-D. |

Do not restrict the qualifying set to four, drop Y from the semantic answer,
or add another menu slot. This is already part of the Listed/Exhaustive
distinction, not an additional research question. Letter-set formatting in the
table is illustrative; use the frozen serialization for generated examples.

Final answers contain letters only: one letter for Unique, a nonempty subset
of A-D for ordinary Listed/Exhaustive answers, or E alone or F alone.
Never combine E/F with each other or with A-D. No reason codes or canonical
full-set payloads are required in the final answer. Explain the reason in the
reasoning trace; retain precise semantic cases, escape reasons, and full
canonical answer sets in audit metadata.

This supersedes the five-option, `E: reason`, and canonical-only proposals.
Exhaustive scoring is **complete-set selection with an escape**, not generation
of missing values. F does not demonstrate knowledge of the unlisted set.
Unique E groups uncertainty, inconsistency, and Selection no-match or
multiple-match failures of uniqueness by design. It does not necessarily mean
uncertainty, and the letter alone cannot distinguish these reasons.

Shuffle substantive values across A-D and compute answer letters afterward.
Use identical permutations for matched Natural/Symbolic examples and matched
contract views with the same menu. For paired coverage variants, keep shared
values in the same slots where feasible. Balance substantive correct-answer
positions across the dataset; E and F stay fixed as intentional interface rules.
Menus must contain four valid distinct values from the declared answer domain;
freeze admissible candidate/domain sizes rather than padding with fake values.
Freeze exact letter-set serialization and parser rules before training; the
new decoder must support A-F and enforce the contract's selection rules.

## Question Templates

Keep spatial assumptions, valid-map semantics, E/F rules, and answer formatting
in a shared instruction block. A valid map satisfies every premise and the
declared spatial assumptions; "could" means in at least one valid map.
Unique requires one invariant answer. For Unique Selection, explicitly state:
"Select E when the premises do not establish exactly one qualifying answer,
including when no object or multiple objects qualify."

Declare eligible objects for Selection and Count independently of the menu.
Removing an option must not change that domain. Unique Count requests an
invariant exact count, not a minimum or maximum. Selection possibilities
describe individually possible objects, not necessarily simultaneous matches;
Count possibilities require joint feasibility in a single valid map.

| Family | Contract | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Unique | In which direction is A relative to B in every valid map? |
| Direction | Listed | Which listed directions could A occupy relative to B? Select all that apply. |
| Direction | Exhaustive | Select the complete set of directions A could occupy relative to B. |
| Selection | Unique | Which eligible object is the only one north of B in every valid map? |
| Selection | Listed | Which listed eligible objects could be north of B? Select all that apply. |
| Selection | Exhaustive | Select the complete set of eligible objects that could be north of B. |
| Count | Unique | What exact number of eligible objects are north of B in every valid map? |
| Count | Listed | Which listed counts are possible for the number of eligible objects north of B? Select all that apply. |
| Count | Exhaustive | Select the complete set of possible counts of eligible objects north of B. |

Substitute the actual object names and queried direction. A-D contain direction
values, eligible object names, or integers according to the family. Keep
"listed" versus "complete set" explicit; do not put solution steps into prompts.

## Dataset Descriptors

Use descriptive terms for this study; retain `L/X/G` only for historical
experiments rather than silently redefining them.

| Descriptor | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Chain length** | Number of premise links along a supporting axis path; record x- and y-path lengths separately when they differ. |
| **Verified distractor count** | Number of premises in a checked set removable individually and together without changing consistency or the complete semantic answer. |
| **Semantic case** | Consistent and uniquely determined, consistent but underdetermined, or inconsistent; retain Selection no-match and multiple-certain-match distinctions where applicable. |

A supporting path is not necessarily the shortest required path. Do not claim
minimum reasoning complexity unless verified, or force a chain length onto a
case without an applicable supporting path. Premises unused by one proof are
not automatically distractors. Verify removability against the full semantic
answer, not merely the displayed menu or final status token.

Semantic case is a category, not an easy-to-hard ranking. Keep query type and
answer contract separate. Also distinguish possible-answer count from
returned-answer count: partial menus can make them differ, and neither alone
measures difficulty. Include Selection no-match and multiple-certain-match
cases, retaining their metadata even when they share an escape letter.

### Controlled Depth Cohort

For the new depth-controlled cohort, additionally measure shortest supporting
paths in the declared atomic axis-graph calculus, with full-premise solver
verification. Record x/y lengths separately; the Direction depth band is their
maximum. Recompute after adding distractors, and reject a requested depth if
shorter axis supports exist. This is a calculus-relative path measure, not
minimum semantic proof size, number of explanation lines, or human difficulty.

For Selection and Count, record per-candidate membership/exclusion supports.
Start depth-cohort feasibility with fixed-membership cases whose entire
candidate set has supported inclusion/exclusion evidence. Aggregate their
measured membership depths by maximum, and check that the nominated deep
support affects the full semantic query answer, not just an auxiliary claim
or the final menu letter. Count still requires joint solver verification.
Unsupported exclusions and invariant counts with changing member identities
must not receive an invented aggregate depth.

Ambiguity, joint-variable Count, contradictions and other nonapplicable cases
remain in the semantic-coverage cohort with a null depth and explicit reason.
They do not satisfy numerical depth quotas. Report depth-cohort accuracy by
query and contract, distinguishing substantive full-menu views from escapes.
Changing menu coverage must not change the underlying depth annotation.

Require approved minimum counts of distinct underlying questions and families
at each supported query/depth cell in train/dev/in-range test (1-8), and in
extrapolation test (9-10). Do not count contract/menu copies toward those minima.
Measure and report object count, candidate count, premise count and verified
distractors alongside depth; longer chains can increase other difficulty
factors, so do not claim a perfectly isolated causal effect of depth.

## Matched Explanation Design

Construct one verified proof or evidence record and render it in two styles.
For definite direction answers, use: **interpret premises, compose axis
constraints, state the direction and encode the menu answer**. Natural uses ordinary
sentences describing coordinate order; Symbolic uses predicates and explicit
axis comparisons. Neither introduces distances, unit steps, or "farther" wording.

Example input: A is northeast of B; C is south of B. Query: A relative to C.
Contract: Unique Answer. Shared menu: A. West; B. Northeast; C. South;
D. Northwest; E. Cannot be determined; F. None of the above.

**Natural**
```text
A has a greater x-coordinate than B and a greater y-coordinate.
C has the same x-coordinate as B and a smaller y-coordinate.
A's x-coordinate is greater than B's, which equals C's.
A's y-coordinate is greater than B's, which is greater than C's.
Therefore, A is northeast of C.
Answer: B
```

**Symbolic**
```text
NE(A,B) => xA > xB; yA > yB
S(C,B)  => xC = xB; yC < yB
xA > xB = xC => xA > xC
yA > yB > yC => yA > yC
=> NE(A,C)
Answer: B
```

No separate `S(C,B) => N(B,C)` rewrite is needed here. Both styles expose
the same deductions; do not compare an abbreviated Natural conclusion against
a fully expanded Symbolic proof and attribute the difference only to notation.

Repeated unconditional axis support may be stated once as a numbered comparison
and reused by its endpoints in both styles. Keep the original full paths and
premise indices in audit metadata; independently expand printed references back
to those links. Never reuse temporary assumptions outside their scope or treat
a shorter displayed derivation as a reduction in measured premise-path depth.
The bounded implementation and comparison policy are documented in the
[selection and prose refinement protocol](research-analysis/SELECTION-AND-PROSE-REFINEMENT-PROTOCOL-v1.md).

Evidence must fit the answer: uniqueness needs its justification, ambiguity
needs differing valid maps, possibility sets need witnesses for included values
and exclusion checks over the contract's domain, and inconsistency needs a
conflicting subset. Retain full verification evidence in audit metadata and
match the evidence selected for supervision across styles. These are generated
explanations, not claims about the model's internal thought process.

For possibility evidence, keep complete numerical witnesses in audit metadata
only. Natural describes relevant spatial relationships or coordinate orders;
Symbolic expresses the same qualitative relationships with predicates or axis
chains. Label these as possible alternatives, not consequences that hold in
every arrangement. Every displayed alternative must have a checked completion
satisfying all premises and the distinct-point axiom. For Count, the described
memberships must hold jointly in one such completion, including nonmembers.
Describe a shared alternative once and refer to it consistently across candidates.
Do not replace checked alternatives with unsupported claims that either outcome
"could happen."

model, adapter setup, example order, and training schedule matched. Only the
reasoning representation and any necessary style instruction differ.
Ambiguous and possibility cases need matched witness-based explanations too;
Symbolic supervision must not omit evidence required of Natural supervision.

Use one shared training seed initially. Compare untuned, Natural, and Symbolic;
no additional answer-only arm, old logical-proof baseline, or hyperparameter
sweep is required. Equal example exposure need not mean equal token compute:
record target-token counts and actual training cost for both arms.

Use the evaluation protocol below. Select each adapter's checkpoint by its
highest **Stage-1 development answer accuracy**, using the frozen final-answer
parser and the adapter's matching style instruction. Missing or invalid final
answers count as incorrect; explanation quality and training loss do not replace
answer accuracy for selection. Use only the held-out synthetic development set,
never test data or Corr.

For the amended design, development contains only in-range depth-cohort cases
(1-8) plus the declared non-depth semantic cases. Depth-9/10 benchmark examples
must never select checkpoints, prompts, hyperparameters or admission settings.

Use the same selected checkpoint for both Stage-1 and Stage-2 test evaluation.
Stage-2 scores must not influence checkpoint selection, and do not select a
separate checkpoint for each stage. Freeze the checkpoint evaluation schedule,
development-score aggregation, and deterministic tie-break rule before training.
Keep frozen Corr SINGLE separate from newly versioned contract variants.

## Evaluation Protocol

For the new study, evaluate four checkpoint/instruction combinations:

| Checkpoint | Stage-1 style instruction |
|---|---|
| Natural adapter | Natural, matching its training style. |
| Symbolic adapter | Symbolic, matching its training style. |
| Untuned base | Natural. |
| Untuned base | Symbolic. |

These are two trained adapters and two evaluations of the same untuned
checkpoint, not four training arms. Compare each adapter with its style-matched
untuned baseline. Keep questions, menus, and contract instructions matched;
only the requested explanation style differs. The adapter-to-adapter comparison
therefore measures each trained system with its intended style instruction.

1. **Stage 1:** allow up to 4,096 generated tokens for the initial reasoning/
   answer response, with the same cap across all four combinations. Preserve
   the raw output, any final answer, and the finish/truncation status.
2. **Stage 2:** run on every example, including valid and truncated Stage-1
   outputs. The same checkpoint receives the original question and its Stage-1
   output, followed by a short answer-completion instruction:
   "Based on the question and your reasoning above, give only the final answer."
   Use structured decoding for one A-D letter under Unique, a nonempty A-D
   subset under Listed/Exhaustive, or E alone or F alone.
3. **Interpretation:** Stage 2 is constrained answer completion, not pure
   extraction, deliberate review, or independent verification. It may finish
   an incomplete decision or change an existing answer. Structured decoding
   constrains format, not correctness or agreement with Stage 1.

Report Stage-1 and Stage-2 accuracy as separate, equally main results; do not
blend them into one score or replace a Stage-1 failure with a Stage-2 success.
Missing/invalid final answers count as failures under the frozen Stage-1 parser,
with format compliance reported separately.

Training explicitly supervises a parseable final answer, so adapters may need
less Stage-2 formatting assistance than untuned. This does not guarantee that
Stage 2 has no benefit: truncation, invalid outputs, and answer revisions may
remain. Measure its gains and regressions for every evaluated combination
rather than assuming training eliminates them.

Before running, freeze the exact style prompts, Stage-1 stop rules, parser,
Stage-2 serialization/grammar, short answer-token allowance, decoding settings,
and context capacity. The replayed question and full Stage-1 output must fit
with the completion allowance; do not silently truncate context. These
implementation settings remain to be specified and tested. The frozen legacy
Corr SINGLE protocol is unchanged by this new-study design.

## Measurements

- Answer accuracy on consistent cases by contract, query type, and
  determinate/underdetermined status.
- Depth-cohort accuracy at each depth, reporting in-range 1-8 and extrapolation
  9-10 separately for both stages. Give distinct base/family counts and applicable
  evidence definitions; do not mix null-depth semantic cases into depth scores.
  Report the training-selected contract-majority baseline as a diagnostic, not
  an additional trained arm or evidence of explanation correctness.
- Substantive-answer performance and E/F selection accuracy separately, sliced
  by gold semantic case and escape reason. Full-set generation is not measured
  by an Exhaustive F answer; detailed reason correctness belongs to explanation
  analysis, not letter-only scoring.
- Performance on inconsistent cases and matched consistent-cycle controls,
  plus conflicting-subset validity in explanations. Unique E accuracy alone
  is not a measure of distinguishing inconsistency from uncertainty.
- Stage-1 generated tokens and total Stage-1 plus Stage-2 generated tokens as
  separate main cost results, across all examples including incorrect and
  truncated outputs. Distinguish reasoning tokens from final-answer tokens
  where reliably measurable. Also record input tokens for both passes,
  including the Stage-1 output replayed as Stage-2 input; do not double-count
  replayed tokens as newly generated tokens. Token savings are not runtime savings.
- Answer-format compliance and reasoning validity where a verifier supports
  it. Report verifier coverage; easier symbolic parsing is not itself evidence
  of better reasoning.
- Stage-1/Stage-2 rescues, regressions, and answer changes, with results split
  by truncated versus completed Stage-1 responses. Report paired Natural-versus-
  Symbolic differences on the same base IDs for both stages. Multiple views
  are not independent examples.

Define an acceptable accuracy-loss tolerance before viewing results.
Small samples and one seed may support only preliminary evidence of accuracy
preservation, not statistical equivalence or seed-robust conclusions.
Freeze matched explanation templates and measure with the actual model
tokenizer; symbolic notation is not automatically shorter, even at greater
chain depth. Exploratory counts from earlier wording are not study results.

Chain-of-Symbol motivates this comparison but studied prompting, not matched
Natural/Symbolic SFT; its cited token savings concern demonstration reasoning.
SPARTQA already uses open-world spatial reasoning. Position the contribution
around the dataset's controlled contract comparisons and checkable evidence,
not novelty of open-world reasoning alone. See the
[primary-source research note](research-analysis/PIVOT-LITERATURE.md).

## Research Integrity

This pivot is post-hoc and informed by inspected Corr failures. Disclose that
history. Do not train on Corr examples, repeatedly tune against its scores,
or silently revise its labels/protocol. Retain prior results in the archive;
they need not become a research question or a full comparison in the new study.

**Working contribution:** SpatialEntail generates controlled spatial problems
with verifiable derivations, counterexamples, and conflict evidence across three
answer contracts. A supporting study compares the accuracy and token cost of
